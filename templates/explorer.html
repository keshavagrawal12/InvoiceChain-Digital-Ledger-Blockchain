Blockchain Explorer

Each bank keeps its own copy of the chain. Every block stores the hash of the block before it, so changing any old block breaks the links after it.

What is this page for?

The Bank Portal is where banks do things. This page lets you look inside the shared ledger and prove it can't be secretly changed.

  1. See the ledger. Each card below is one block. Block #1, #2… each hold the invoices financed when a bank clicked Mine Block. Click a card to see which invoices, which bank, and how much.
  2. See that all banks agree. Switch between the three bank tabs. Each tab is that bank's copy of the same ledger, so all three show every block, whichever bank mined it.
  3. See why it is tamper-proof. Click Tamper Demo to secretly change an amount in an old block, as a dishonest bank employee might. Then click Validate Chain: the edited block turns red, because its contents no longer match its hash.
  4. Repair it. Sync Nodes replaces the broken copy with the valid copy that the other banks hold.

Network validation

Click Validate Chain to recompute every hash, check the previous-hash links, the proof-of-work and the Merkle roots on all three banks.

Chain

Why does every bank show the same blocks — even ones mined by other banks? This is on purpose, and it is the whole point of the project. The tabs are not "each bank's own transactions"; they are each bank's own copy of one shared ledger. When any bank mines a block, it is broadcast to all three, so every copy ends up identical.
  • Fraud check needs it: No Coincidence Bank can only say "Already financed by Bank of BrokeChain" because Bank of BrokeChain's block is in No Coincidence Bank's copy too. If each bank only kept its own blocks, we'd be back to the original problem: every bank blind to what the others financed.
  • Tamper-proof needs it: because three identical copies exist, a change to one copy stands out (try Tamper Demo) and can be repaired from the other two (Sync Nodes).
  • Who financed what? Click a block. The "Bank" column in its records shows which bank financed each invoice.
